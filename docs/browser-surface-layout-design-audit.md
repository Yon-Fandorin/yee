# Service Design Audit: Browser Surface structural states

## Intent

- Purpose: keep Chromium-native notices, page surfaces, split panes, and overlays inside Yee's intended Browser Surface without changing their native models.
- User: desktop browser user navigating or recovering from a browser notice — tension: native chrome must remain trustworthy and actionable while the shell changes shape.
- Primary job: navigate
- Trust level: browser chrome boundaries, warnings, focus, and page ownership must be unambiguous.
- High-stakes: no
- Primary viewport(s): restored/maximized desktop on macOS, Windows, and Linux; minimum supported desktop width (support-only: content fullscreen and unusual fractional-scale combinations until real-app coverage is available)
- Scope: single/split, expanded/collapsed Sidebar, InfoBar, native Side Panel, page overlays, small panes, animations, fullscreen/immersive, RTL, theme, scale, keyboard, and accessibility states.

Chromium source references below are checkout-relative path identifiers. Line
anchors are intentionally omitted because the generated checkout is Git-ignored
and line numbers drift; the tracked Chromium glue is reconstructed from
[`0001-integrate-yee-shell.patch`](../chromium-overlay/patches/0001-integrate-yee-shell.patch).

## Lens coverage

- product-intent: examined — one job is clear: preserve native browser UI while Yee owns presentation bounds; F3 captures the missing structural means.
- flow-continuity: F2, F17 — InfoBar flow is lost and manager/pane state can be non-atomic; entry/exit, promotion, pane switch, detach, and generation continuity are acceptance gates.
- information-architecture: examined — surface roles are now divided into structural, pane/page, and window-global scopes with no duplicate finding.
- layout-composition: F1, F23 — independent rectangles and discrete-state Toolbar placement visibly break the Surface boundary.
- interaction-model: F6, F18, F19, F25 — page/window/shared/tab scopes, parent changes, controller topology, and idempotent mode teardown need distinct containment and lifecycle rules.
- forms-input: examined — n/a; no form is introduced, while native InfoBar controls retain Chromium behavior.
- feedback-edge-states: F4, F13 — split notices and every current Side Panel/underlap/animation state need explicit geometry.
- efficiency-expert-use: examined — native accelerators and repeated browsing commands remain unchanged; no new repeat-task cost is introduced.
- responsive-behavior: F8, F10, F11, F16 — minimum panes, stacked notices, moved transient UI, and platform fullscreen transitions are explicit state cells.
- platform-convention-fit: F9, F21 — pane-local native UI and browser-class routing must follow the owning native window/pane.
- typography: examined — no type-system change is proposed; existing native InfoBar typography remains, needs-render after implementation.
- color-contrast: examined — no palette-role change; InfoBar semantic colors remain native, needs-render in light/dark/high contrast.
- surface-depth: F5, F20 — duplicate/native shadows, separators, top-corner masks, and z-order cross surface ownership.
- component-consistency: F3, F14, F15 — one Browser Surface concept needs explicit native allocation and pure pane-value seams instead of assumed APIs.
- visual-craft-coherence: F22 — visual tolerance cannot weaken exact native/compositor clips; remaining craft needs rebuilt real-app captures.
- content-microcopy: examined — native Chromium notice copy and actions are preserved.
- trust-risk-consent: examined — warning/notice ownership stays with Chromium; no suppression or replacement is allowed.
- brand-expression: examined — Yee continues to own the chrome frame while semantic native notices remain recognizable.
- accessibility-inclusion: F12, F24 — the design preserves the native InfoBar parent and moves Chromium's visible action identifier with the Sidebar Toggle host.
- perceived-performance: F7 — current and target geometry must not diverge visibly during Sidebar/Side Panel animation.

## Implementation checkpoint

- I1: F1, F2, F4, F12, and F21 have implementation and automated build/test evidence, but remain `built` until their full state matrix is independently verified.
- I2: F23-F25 were discovered during implementation. Single/split collapse and expand automated flows complete, the current Header edge was wired into Toolbar placement, and old TabStrip reset is guarded before teardown. I6 later reopens F23 because real transition pixels contradict the automated geometry verdict.
- I3: the visual-validation launcher blocker is fixed. Foreground `run.sh` now activates the Yee app bundle through macOS Launch Services and waits for its lifecycle; a clean temporary profile registers a capturable CGWindow and remains attached until graceful shutdown.
- I4: real-app pixel evidence now covers single-pane expanded/collapsed, two real tabs in split expanded/collapsed, and Browser-level InfoBar presentations. At the 1171 px captured window width, the observed horizontal contracts were approximately: single expanded `229…1166`, single collapsed `5…1166`, split expanded active right pane `702…1166`, and split collapsed active right pane `591…1166`. The InfoBar appeared once in the active right pane, stayed below its Pane Header, preserved its action/close controls, and never crossed the divider, Sidebar, or outer edge. The exact PDF copy was later triggered directly through `chrome://infobar-internals`; `pdf-infobar-exact-expanded-sidebar.jpeg` confirms its message, action, close control, and horizontal containment at 768×874 with the Sidebar expanded.
- I5: the feasible real-app matrix was extended to native fullscreen, 800×600, dark mode, RTL, forced scale 1.25, and Find Bar. The Browser Surface, split divider, Pane Headers, and Sidebar reservation remained bounded in the final fullscreen round trip and in 800×600 expanded/collapsed split captures. F10 failed at 800×600 with the Sidebar expanded: the native Find Bar retained its preferred width and crossed from the active pane into the Yee Sidebar in both LTR light mode and RTL dark/scale-1.25 mode. Normal-width and collapsed-Sidebar Find Bar cells passed, which isolates the missing active-pane clipping box rather than a general z-order failure. At this checkpoint, true transition mid-frame pixels, Status Bubble hover, native Side Panel/product-reserved slots, specialized page overlays, and independent third-party re-verification remained open; I6 supersedes the Sidebar-transition part of that statement.
- I6: window-only real-app recordings decoded at original frame timestamps close the Sidebar portion of the transition-pixel gap. Collapse changes were bounded, while expansion failed F23: at the 6.097 s frame the Browser Surface left edge had already advanced into the expanding state but Sidebar Toggle, Back, and Forward remained at their collapsed Header positions inside the gray Sidebar region. The raw expand/collapse recordings, contact sheets, and full-resolution failure frame are retained under `browser-surface-validation/`. InfoBar and fullscreen mid-frame coverage remains open because Computer Use repeatedly returned `cgWindowNotFound` after the required graceful restart; no pass was inferred from that tooling failure.
- I7: F23 was traced to a layout-lifecycle seam rather than the Header rectangle calculation.
